Step three  /  Explain

One law, three ways

Every panel below says exactly the same thing. If one of them is hard to follow, start with a different one and come back to it.

In words

The EMF induced in a coil is directly proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic flux linkage. A field sitting still induces nothing. Only a changing field induces an EMF.

In symbols

ε = −N ΔΦ / Δt

ε induced EMF, in volts
N number of turns on the coil
ΔΦ change in flux, in weber
Δt time taken, in seconds
− the induced current opposes the change

In a picture

Going in, the flux grows and the EMF is positive. At the centre the flux stops growing for an instant and the EMF passes through zero. Coming out, the flux shrinks and the EMF reverses.

Key words for this lesson

Write each word in the language you think in. You will remember it better than the English on its own.

Magnetic flux How much magnetic field passes through the coil. Symbol Φ, unit weber.
Induction Making a current appear in a wire without touching it, using a changing field.
Electromotive force The push that drives the induced current. Symbol ε, unit volt.
Rate of change How fast something is changing, not how big it is. This is the whole lesson.

Step four  /  Check

Three questions, examination style

2 marks

Question one

State Faraday's Law of electromagnetic induction in words.

The EMF induced in a circuit is directly proportional to the rate of change of the magnetic flux linkage with the circuit.

Marker note. The words rate of change carry the mark. An answer saying the EMF depends on the magnetic flux, without saying rate of change, does not earn it.
3 marks

Question two

The north pole of a bar magnet is pushed towards a coil. The face of the coil closest to the magnet becomes which pole, and why?

4 marks

Question three

A coil of 250 turns experiences a change in magnetic flux of 3,6 × 10−3 Wb in 0,15 s. Calculate the magnitude of the induced EMF.

ε = N ΔΦ / Δt
ε = 250 × (3,6 × 10−3) / 0,15
ε = 250 × 0,024
ε = 6,0 V
